## The Key Difference (Simple but Powerful)

Intuition is quiet.

Overthinking is loud.

Intuition feels like:

- a calm knowing  
- a simple “yes” or “no”  
- no need to explain everything  

Overthinking feels like:

- endless analysis  
- urgency to decide  
- fear of making the wrong choice  

One feels steady.

The other feels restless.

---

## Why Overthinking Gets Mistaken for Intuition

Because both involve attention.

And both feel important.

But overthinking is driven by:

- fear of regret  
- fear of judgment  
- fear of missing out  

So your brain keeps searching.

Trying to protect you from a “wrong” decision.

But instead—

it creates more noise.

---

## The Comparison Trap (Where It Gets Worse)

You open social media.

Everyone seems ahead.

More certain.  
More successful.  
More confident.

So your thoughts shift:

- *Maybe I’m behind*  
- *Maybe I’m choosing wrong*  
- *Maybe I should be doing what they’re doing*  

Now your decision isn’t yours anymore.

It’s influenced by comparison.

And comparison feeds overthinking.

This loop is similar to [catastrophizing patterns](/category/spiraling/catastrophizing-stop), where your brain escalates uncertainty into something bigger than it is.

And it often overlaps with [feeling drained after work](/category/burnout/drained-after-work), where mental fatigue makes decisions feel heavier than they are.

## Overthinking vs Intuition (Side by Side)

| Intuition | Overthinking |
|------|------|
| Quiet | Loud |
| Simple | Complicated |
| Calm | Urgent |
| Clear feeling | Confused thoughts |
| Doesn’t need proof | Needs constant reassurance |

If it feels overwhelming—

it’s probably not intuition.

## How to Not Overthink (Without Forcing It)

You don’t need to “turn off” your thoughts.

You just need to stop following all of them.

---

### 1. Step Away Before Deciding

Clarity rarely comes mid-spiral.

Pause.

Walk away.

Let the noise settle first.

---

### 2. Notice the Energy Behind the Thought

Ask:

- Is this coming from fear?  
- Or from calm awareness?  

That difference matters more than the thought itself.

---

### 3. Limit Comparison Inputs

Less scrolling = less confusion.

Not because others are wrong—

but because their path isn’t yours.

---

### 4. Give Yourself a “No-Decision Window”

Not everything needs to be decided immediately.

Pressure creates noise.

Time creates clarity.

---

### 5. Trust Simplicity

If something feels quietly right—

you don’t need to over-explain it.

---

## Finding Glimmers (Instead of Comparing)

Instead of chasing certainty—

look for small moments that feel *right*.

Not big answers.

Just small signals.

Glimmers can be:

- a moment of calm  
- a sense of ease  
- a decision that feels lighter  

They don’t shout.

They don’t demand attention.

They just… feel okay.

And that’s enough.
