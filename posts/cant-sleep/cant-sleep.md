## What Does Can’t Sleep Actually Mean?

When you say you *can’t sleep*, it doesn’t always mean insomnia.

In many cases, it means you’re **delaying sleep on purpose**, even when your body is tired.

This is known as *revenge bedtime procrastination*—a behavior where people stay awake late to reclaim personal time.

According to research on [sleep deprivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_deprivation), chronic lack of sleep affects emotional regulation, focus, and mental health.

Psychological research also suggests that perceived loss of control during the day increases the likelihood of delayed sleep behavior (see https://www.psychologytoday.com/).

So the real issue isn’t just sleep.

It’s the **emotional pattern behind why you can’t sleep**.

## Why This Happens (Science Explained)

From a neuroscience perspective, *can’t sleep* behavior like revenge bedtime procrastination ADHD: the "dopamine" trap explained is driven by **dopamine imbalance**.

According to behavioral psychology research, intermittent rewards (like scrolling or short videos) strongly activate the brain’s reward system.

For people with ADHD tendencies:

- Lower baseline dopamine → stronger craving for stimulation  
- Nighttime freedom → increased sense of control  
- Instant content → rapid dopamine spikes  

This creates a loop:

> Exhaustion → delay sleep → dopamine reward → regret → repeat  

Over time, your brain learns:

> “Nighttime = the only time I feel good.”

That’s why you can’t sleep—even when you desperately want to.

## 5 Small Things You Can Do Right Now

They’re **micro-adjustments that weaken the dopamine loop**.
